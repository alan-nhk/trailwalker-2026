01

It began with an idea

2024 — 2026 · Before the start
Chapter 01

An idea

In 2024 the four of us started planning this. Back then none of us could really run; a hundred kilometres was just a number that sounded very far away.

At a gathering someone said: why don't we do Trailwalker? I said yes on the spot. Saying yes is easy — a word, a nod, not even a second.

But when it came to actually entering, I backed out. A hundred kilometres is not a short walk. I asked myself: can I finish? And if I don't, then what? The fear was very concrete — concrete enough that you would rather not start.

So in 2025, I didn't enter.

Chapter 02

The year it didn't happen

In 2025, after the fire at Wang Fuk Court, the event was finally called off. Places, training, plans — everything stopped where it stood.

I remember not feeling especially relieved, and not especially disappointed. Only that some things are not ours to decide.

Chapter 03

Reserve

In 2026 the event returned. A teammate dropped out, and a place opened up.

By then I had been preparing for a while — not to fill someone's shoes, but because I finally wanted to try. To find out whether the person who was too afraid to enter in 2025 could actually do it.

So I joined officially as the reserve and became the fourth person. And then we took the first step of those hundred kilometres.

Chapter 04

A difficult start

Training did not go as smoothly as we imagined. Halfway through, the captain broke his arm; it hung against his chest in a sling. When you can't even put on your own backpack without help, we did wonder: do we postpone again?

We had planned a 50 km race as a rehearsal. In the end we couldn't go.

Before the event there was no time left. The four of us could no longer train together; we trained separately, checked in, and kept an eye on each other — did you run today? Were you on the hill last week?

Four people, four schedules. The only thing we shared was the same date.

02

Standing on the start line

20 Nov 2026 · Start
Chapter 05

The start line, in November

And then, at last, all four of us were standing on the start line.

The moment we stepped onto it, another challenge appeared. The weather turned — the conditions were worse than we imagined, worse than we had prepared for.

Worse still, we had no idea there was no bag drop. The four of us had to deal with our luggage before the start, and the plan for the support points collapsed with it. Where each checkpoint was, who carried what, what time they would arrive — all of it had to be worked out again.

We could have chosen not to start. But we were already on the line, so we decided: start first, work it out step by step.

Chapter 06

Mud

After the start the weather got worse. Rain ran down the trail, and the earth turned to mud.

Every step was slippery; every step you had to think about where to put your foot. We went carefully, but you cannot avoid the occasional fall — and when you go down, your whole leg is mud.

One of us began to show signs of hypothermia, shivering without stopping. We kept warm the simplest way we could: more layers, a slow jog, letting the body make its own heat. Jogging to stay warm, taking the cold, one step at a time towards the first support point.

At the support point we bought some food, let our bodies warm up, and set off again.

Chapter 07

Kei Ling Ha

It was at Kei Ling Ha that our support crew appeared.

Only then did things really turn around. Hot food, fresh food, proper care for our bodies; and warming gear, so that we could be warm for a while.

Someone handed you something hot. Only then did you realise how cold you had been.

03

The coldest night

20 — 21 Nov 2026 · Night
Chapter 08

The coldest day in Hong Kong

After dark the temperature dropped again. We heard it was the coldest day of the year in Hong Kong.

Cold and exhausted, it is very easy to fall asleep. All of us were a little hazy, following one another slowly. A few times I lost consciousness for a moment; walking on, the body would sway.

I remember that night very clearly, and not at all.

“Four people, one behind the other.
Nobody spoke.
Nobody stopped.” 20 November · Late night
Chapter 09

Sunrise

When the sun slowly came up, we began to wake properly, and felt a little more human.

The organisers started giving out hot drinks, and many selfless sponsors and suppliers brought better food. Once something hot was inside you, you belonged to yourself again.

04

From winter to summer in a day

21 Nov 2026 · Day two
Chapter 10

The second day's sun

The second morning was warm. But nobody expected the afternoon sun to be so fierce — the whole mountain turned into summer.

In a single day we went through extreme cold and extreme heat. The night before we were shivering; the next day we were looking for shade.

Luckily we had looked after ourselves along the way, and none of us ran a fever. We all had a bit of a cold, but nothing serious, so we could keep going.

Chapter 11

Blisters

By the middle and later sections, the muscles had stopped cooperating.

The captain was injured; a mild pain had set in, and he could only move slowly. Everyone was getting blisters, and our pace kept dropping.

And at the same time, the wish to be at the finish grew more urgent. The more urgent you feel, the more tired you get.

“The hardest part of a hundred kilometres
is not the climb.
It is knowing how slow you are,
and going on anyway.”
Chapter 12

Tai Mo Shan

Then came the biggest gate of all: Tai Mo Shan.

It is a slope with no visible end. When we reached the top, the four of us lay down on the grass and nobody wanted to speak. The wind was strong, the sky was blue, and we had just put the highest mountain in Hong Kong under our feet.

Taken.

Chapter 13

Rotary Park

Coming down from Tai Mo Shan to Rotary Park, the second support crew appeared.

They were full of energy, and walked a stretch with us. Because they were there, our tired bodies and our mood picked up a little joy, and a little strength.

It turns out that when someone is waiting for you, the road gets shorter.

21 November · The finish
32:55Hours · Minutes

At the finish, everyone was overcome — and none of us quite believed we had really done those hundred kilometres. I think that was the most exhilarating moment of the whole thing. The road was unforgettable; and it was finally over, and the body could rest for a moment.

Afterword

We could never have guessed
that the weather
would turn this savage.

Looking back, the whole thing was very hard. Before we set off we checked the forecast; it was supposed to be sunny. Then a cold front arrived, and everything changed.

And because of that challenge, these hundred kilometres are all the more unforgettable.

Finally, thank you once more to everyone who fundraised and everyone who donated. Because of you, we finished. And we hope that through this event, these donations reach the people who need them.
